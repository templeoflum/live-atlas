After closing the missing-card queue, review **every mapped card** against its

Completion gate: every source topic has a useful, discoverable local home;
essential explanations are present without an external-manual visit; no topic
requires Find as its only route; no extra explanation foldouts are introduced.
Use original concise synthesis, not copied manual paragraphs.

## 2. Place every manual image

Begin only after the text/card structure is complete. Inventory every figure in
the cached manual body with its chapter, section, caption and source URL. Map it
to the exact card/action it explains, including secondary controls. Reuse a
figure where several actions genuinely need it; do not dump figures into a
chapter-wide gallery or use a generic screenshot to fill a gap.

Check resolution, aspect ratio, clipping, readable labels, enlargement, small
screens, alt text, captions, attribution and any distribution requirements.
Every manual figure gets an explicit placement; record duplicate illustrations
without silently dropping their relevant contexts. Custom stills and short
action loops come later, for needs the manual’s imagery does not meet.

## 3. Deepen Live’s History

Begin after the full manual and image layers are in place. Build a substantial
history of Live itself: musical beginnings, people and collaborators, early
versions, major releases, Session/Arrangement and warping, MIDI and instruments,
Racks, Max for Live, interface decisions, and Push’s development.

Research primary interviews, release announcements, talks, documentation,
archived interfaces and direct accounts. Distinguish documented motivations
from interpretation. Show dates, people, concrete design decisions and what
changed in the instrument; do not force every story into a present-day lesson.
Use period-appropriate illustrations and keep the section exploratory rather
than turning it into one long essay. The current short timeline is a starting
point, not the completed history.
